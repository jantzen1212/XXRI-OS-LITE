## 2. The transparency question, answered with measurements

The brief asked me not to conclude "impossible" without research. What I found:

**No compositor exists for this platform.** The Tiny Core 16.x repository was
enumerated (2694 packages): it ships `libXcomposite`, `libXdamage`, `libXfixes`
and `libXrender`, but **no `xcompmgr`, `compton`, `picom` or `transset`**.

Building `xcompmgr` from source against the existing i686 sysroot is genuinely
feasible — every library it needs is present. **I decided against it, and this
is a judgement call rather than a limitation:** XXRI targets Pentium-III-class
machines with no GPU acceleration (the mockup's own About page reads "Intel
Pentium III / SiS 6326 Integrated graphic"). Software XRender compositing of
every window on that hardware trades the whole desktop's responsiveness for a
cosmetic effect, and it would put the 10.2 stability baseline at risk. The brief
ranks stability above effects, so the effect was obtained another way.

> **CORRECTION (Phase 10.4).** Two things here turned out to be wrong. The
> §8 claim that the server offers "0 ARGB visuals" came from counting
> `depth: 32` lines in `xdpyinfo`, which counted the wrong thing: a proper
> probe reports **10 ARGB visuals with alpha masks**, and
> `XCompositeGetOverlayWindow` is granted. And the performance judgement was
> too pessimistic — a *damage-driven* compositor costs about 0.1% idle CPU and
> 2 MB here, because it does nothing while nothing changes. Phase 10.4 wrote a
> small XXRI-specific compositor and real alpha now works. See
> PHASE10.4-REPORT.md.

**What was used instead — pseudo-transparency, in two forms:**

1. **The dock** (`wbar-bg.png`). Measurement first: sampling the pill against
   the wallpaper across its width showed the colour tracking the wallpaper, which
   proves wbar already alpha-composites its background image over the root. The
   pill was therefore rebuilt as *translucency* rather than paint — a vertical
   alpha ramp over a white→lavender colour ramp, with a bright hairline rim just
   inside the edge. Generated by `work/make-dock-bg.sh`, tuned against the mockup
   with `work/dock-sim.sh`, which composites a candidate over the real wallpaper
   at the real dock geometry (a one-second preview instead of a five-minute boot).

2. **The Control Center**, which already sampled `_XROOTPMAP_ID` for frosted
   glass, now also gets a **drop shadow** — see §3.

## 3. A drop shadow without a compositor

The window shape mask is one-bit: a pixel is in the window or it is not, so a
soft outer edge looks impossible. The trick is to make the hard edge *invisible*
instead of soft. The window is grown by 12px on every side and that margin is
painted with **the wallpaper itself**, darkened most next to the panel and fading
to completely untouched wallpaper at the outer boundary. The outermost painted
pixel is identical to the pixel that would have been there anyway, so the mask
edge cannot be seen, and everything inside it reads as a soft shadow. The blur is
faked with concentric rounded rectangles of decreasing alpha.

`debug/phase10.3/AB-control-center-shadow.png` (before / after).

**This introduced a real bug, which was caught by looking at the screenshot.**
With an application window behind the panel, the "shadow" painted bright blue
wallpaper over a dark terminal — a glowing halo (see the composition test). The
effect had stopped reading as translucency and started reading as a defect. The
fix is that the panel now *asks what is behind it*: `something_behind()` walks
the root's children for a viewable, non-override-redirect window intersecting the
panel rectangle. Over the desktop it draws frosted glass and a shadow; over a
window it drops both and draws an honest solid surface.
`debug/phase10.3/final/v6-over.png` shows the corrected behaviour.

Two fallbacks guard it: no `_XROOTPMAP_ID` published → the window is shaped down
to the panel and simply has no shadow, rather than painting a flat slab.

## 4. Rounded window corners

The mockups draw every window with rounded corners; the desktop had hard
rectangles. flwm now clips each **frame** with the X Shape extension, which
clips the client inside it too, so applications' own square corners disappear
with them. Radius 9px — large enough to read as a curve, small enough that the
unavoidable one-bit stepping is not obvious at native size
(`debug/phase10.3/rounded-window-corner.png`). Rebuilt on geometry change and on
first paint. `XXRI_SQUARE_CORNERS=1` turns it off.

## 6. Animation — one, and only where it is free

The Control Center now **unfolds out of its chip**: the panel is laid out and
painted once at full size, then its *clip region* grows from the chip's pill to
the panel rectangle over 7 frames at 18ms. Nothing is re-laid-out, nothing is
re-rendered, no window is resized — the only thing moving is the shape, so there
is nothing to tear or flicker. A guard suppresses the collapse timer during the
reveal, because a shape smaller than the pointer expects reports a crossing *out*
of the window and would otherwise close the panel the instant it opened.

Stress-tested with 8 rapid open/close cycles: no stuck state, no doubled panel,
no collapse-on-open.

No other animation was added. Window-state transitions and dock-icon zoom would
both need compositing to look right, and the brief ranks a stable static
transition above a broken animated one.

## 8. Visual self-critique — what is still not right

Being honest about what a careful eye still sees:

* **Application windows are opaque.** The mockups show translucent window
  bodies with the wallpaper behind them. Pseudo-transparency cannot honestly do
  this for a full window: windows overlap constantly, and sampling the wallpaper
  where another window actually sits would look broken far more often than it
  looked good — exactly the failure I hit with the Control Center shadow and then
  had to defend against. It stays opaque until there is a compositor.
* **The dock has no drop shadow.** wbar's window *is* the pill, so there is no
  margin outside it to paint one, and adding a second window underneath would
  fight wbar for stacking. The bright rim carries the separation instead.
* **The desktop menu has square corners.** FLTK creates the popup window inside
  `popup()`, so there is nowhere to attach a shape mask without patching FLTK.
  Its palette, frame, glyphs and rhythm are XXRI; its outline is not.
* **The Disks icon is busy at 40px.** I first assumed it was leftover Tiny Core
  artwork and was wrong: `mnttool.png` is byte-identical to the official
  `icons/xxri/64x64/apps/storage.png`, so it is the canonical XXRI icon and was
  left alone. It simply carries more detail than reads at dock size.
* Rounded corners step slightly at the pixel level; unavoidable with a one-bit
  mask.

# Addendum — the livability pass

The critique after the first pass was that the desktop looked *too clean*: a
polished artboard rather than a system someone lives in. That is a fair reading,
and it is a different problem from "does it match the mockup". This pass went
after continuity, state and spatial relationship rather than more decoration.

## Research, and what was actually taken from it

Samsung's own guidance was read rather than guessed at. The two things that
mattered:

* **[One UI motion](https://developer.samsung.com/one-ui/motion/basic.html)** —
  motion exists to "emphasize the depth of screens and help users understand the
  relationship between UI elements". Elements enter and leave along paths that
  come from their context. Duration floor 100ms, ceiling 500ms, standard curve
  accelerate-then-decelerate.
* **[Samsung DeX](https://www.samsung.com/us/support/answer/ANS10002880/)** —
  minimising does not make a window disappear, it *goes to the taskbar*; the
  taskbar carries an indicator for what is running; clicking an icon switches
  focus to that window.

The lesson taken was not "rounded cards". It was **origin**: a window should
come from somewhere and go back to somewhere, and the desktop should make focus
obvious without being asked.

## What changed

### 1. Windows now come from, and return to, their dock icon

`xxri-wctl` publishes each dock icon's rectangle on the root window as
`_XXRI_DOCK_SLOTS`; it is the only process that knows the dock's geometry, and
the window manager is the only one that can move windows, so a root property
lets them cooperate without either knowing the other exists.

flwm then animates **the frame only** — the client inside is never touched, so
no application is asked to re-lay-out mid-flight and the whole thing costs
nothing on a machine with no acceleration. ~170ms, decelerating, per One UI's
range and curve. `XXRI_NO_ANIM=1` disables it.

* **launch** — the window grows out of its dock icon
* **minimize** — the window travels down into its dock icon
* **restore** — it comes back out of it

Proven by building a deliberately slowed copy of the window manager, injecting
it into an image with `debugfs`, and catching the flights in mid-air:
`debug/phase10.3/livability/a2-launch-flight.png`, `a2-min-flight.png`,
`a2-restore-flight.png`.

### 2. Focus is now obvious

Measured first: an unfocused window's controls were rendering at *identical*
saturation to the focused one (`#E624CE / #7B22F0 / #2A22EE` in both), leaving
focus to be inferred from a two-shade difference in the bar. Unfocused controls
now recede toward the bar colour, keeping enough hue that the △ □ X language
still reads, and hovering restores full colour so the control still invites the
click. `livability/focus-obvious-vs-recessed.png`.

### 3. The Control Center retracts by itself — and a real bug behind that

The panel would not close on its own. The trace showed why, and it was not a
timing problem: **no leave event ever arrived**. The corner hotspot sits in the
margin *outside* the panel, so opening it from the corner never generates an
enter on the panel window — and with no enter there can never be a leave. A
panel opened that way waited forever for an event that could not happen.

The retract decision now comes from the pointer sample that already runs for the
hotspot: if the pointer is neither on the panel (with 10px of slack) nor in the
corner, it retracts after 500ms — and it retracts *toward the corner*, playing
the reveal backwards, so the panel visibly returns to where it came from.
Verified in all three states: opens on the corner, **stays open while the
pointer dwells on it** (8s), retracts when the pointer leaves.

### 4. A second bug: the wallpaper effects were misidentifying windows

The bright halo returned. The `something_behind()` guard was testing
`override_redirect` to skip the dock — but **a window manager's frames are
themselves override-redirect**, which is exactly how they avoid being managed.
That test skipped every application on screen, so the panel always believed it
was over bare desktop. It now tests for `WM_STATE`, the property the window
manager puts on what it manages — which excludes the dock and the panel itself
without excluding anything real. Confirmed by trace
(`behind: win 0x200009 986x690+0+0 overlaps panel 264x161+742+587`) and by
`livability/halo-fixed.png`.

A related fix: the surface treatment now repaints when that answer *changes*.
Updating the flag alone left the previous glass on screen.

### 5. Small structural detail, where it clarifies

* the volume row carries its **value** (`70%`) — a slider tells you where the
  handle is, not what it means
* one **hairline** separates the controls you adjust from the controls that act
  on the session; the panel holds two different kinds of thing and one rule is
  enough to say so
* **contextual spacing** replaced the uniform 10px column: 6px base rhythm with
  the connectivity tiles given their own margin. Uniform spacing everywhere is
  a large part of what makes a panel look generated rather than composed

## Self-critique against the brief's own questions

* *Does the dock feel connected to applications?* — Yes: windows come out of and
  return to their icons, and the indicator tracks running/focused/minimized.
* *Does focus feel obvious?* — Yes, now. It did not before, and I could show it
  numerically.
* *Does every click get a reaction?* — Launching flies out of the icon; focusing
  a running app changes its indicator from running to focused; titlebar controls
  have hover and pressed states.
* *Is anything still too perfect?* — The dock is still six identically-sized
  icons at identical spacing, which is correct for a dock. The desktop itself is
  bare; XXRI has no desktop icons by design.
* *Do Settings and Store feel like part of XXRI?* — Partly. Their sidebar already
  carries a gradient, dotted row rules, hover and selected states, so it is a
  layered surface rather than a slab. But their window **bodies are opaque**
  where the mockup shows them translucent, and that stays true until there is a
  compositor: sampling the wallpaper behind a full application window would be
  wrong whenever another window is there — which is exactly the failure this
  pass had to fix twice in the Control Center.
